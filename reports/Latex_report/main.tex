\begin{abstract}
Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) decompose language-model activations into
sparse, often human-interpretable features. We investigate whether SAE
features that receive plausible-sounding auto-generated labels are also
\emph{causally faithful} to those labels -- that is, whether deliberately
strengthening a feature moves model behavior toward the concept its label
describes. We benchmark this with a steering-based evaluation across
multiple SAE architectures (ReLU, TopK, JumpReLU) and models (GPT-2 Small,
Gemma-2-2B).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) are used to explain what language models represent, which presumes that an SAE
reconstruction is a faithful stand-in for the activation it replaces \citep{bricken2023monosemanticity,
cunningham2023sparse, gao2024scaling}. We ask: \emph{can SAE reconstruction of frozen LLM activations be improved
while preserving truth-related information and causal behaviour across transformer layers?} We separate four levels
that are often conflated: (i) reconstruction quality (NMSE, explained variance, cosine, L0); (ii) preservation of task
information (frozen and re-fitted probes on reconstructions); (iii) behavioural faithfulness (splicing the
reconstruction into the forward pass: next-token KL and cross-entropy increase); and (iv) causal faithfulness (how
much of a located causal effect the reconstruction carries). We study GPT-2 Small \citep{radford2019gpt2} at all 12
layers with in-house ReLU, TopK and JumpReLU SAEs, on OpenWebText (OWT), the Geometry of Truth (GoT) true/false
statements \citep{marks2023geometry} and a persona-honesty task derived from RepE \citep{zou2023representation}.


\section{Related Work / Literature Review}
\label{sec:litreview}

This section summarizes prior work most relevant to SAE-Faithful and
states explicitly how our project differs from each.

\subsection{Sparse autoencoders for interpretability}

\citet{cunningham2023sparse} and \citet{bricken2023monosemanticity} showed
that sparse autoencoders trained on language-model activations recover
directions that are substantially more monosemantic than raw neurons,
establishing SAEs as a practical interpretability tool and introducing the
now-standard ReLU SAE formulation we use as one of our three architectures.
\emph{Difference:} both works focus on discovering and qualitatively
describing interpretable features; neither tests whether a feature's label
is \textbf{causally} accurate via steering, which is our central benchmark.

\citet{gao2024scaling} introduced the TopK SAE, which enforces a fixed
number of active features per example instead of relying on an L1 penalty,
improving the accuracy/sparsity tradeoff at scale. \citet{rajamanoharan2024jumping}
introduced JumpReLU SAEs, which use a learned per-feature activation
threshold to improve reconstruction fidelity without sacrificing sparsity.
\emph{Difference:} both papers evaluate architecture quality via
reconstruction loss and downstream-task probing, not via a causal
steering-based faithfulness benchmark comparing labels to behavior -- our
Phase 6 cross-architecture comparison is designed to test whether these
architectural differences translate into different causal faithfulness,
not just different reconstruction quality.

\subsection{Base models and pretrained SAE resources}

\citet{radford2019gpt2} introduced GPT-2, the primary model family (GPT-2
Small) used for our experiments, chosen for its small size and wide
availability of pretrained SAEs. \citet{gemmateam2024gemma2} introduced
Gemma 2, and \citet{gemmascope2024} released Gemma Scope, a suite of
pretrained SAEs across Gemma 2 layers; we use Gemma-2-2B with Gemma Scope
as our secondary, larger-scale comparison model (Research Question 9:
whether GPT-2-Small findings generalize). \emph{Difference:} these are
model/tooling releases rather than faithfulness studies; we build directly
on top of them rather than replicating their contributions.

\citet{saelens2024} (SAELens) and \citet{transformerlens2022}
(TransformerLens) are the libraries used throughout this project to load
models, cache activations, and register the forward hooks used for causal
steering (see \texttt{src/models/gpt2\_sae.py}, \texttt{src/sae/steering.py}).
\citet{neuronpedia} provides independently auto-generated feature labels
that we cross-check our own feature-label findings against (e.g. Feature
974 on GPT-2 Small being independently described as relating to
``mentions of Paris,'' see \texttt{results/phase1\_observations.md}).
\emph{Difference:} we treat Neuronpedia's labels as an independent
cross-check on labels, not as ground truth for causal faithfulness, which
is exactly the gap this project measures.

\subsection{Concept detection and representation-level evaluation}

\citet{marks2023geometry} (Geometry of Truth) show that true/false
statement representations in language models are approximately linearly
separable, and release a dataset of true/false statements we use for
Phase 5 concept-detection experiments (see
\texttt{cookbooks/03\_geometry\_of\_truth\_eda.ipynb}).
\citet{zou2023representation} (Representation Engineering / RepE) propose
extracting behavioral directions (e.g. honesty, harmlessness) from
contrastive example pairs and using them for both interpretation and
control. \emph{Difference:} both works focus on discovering or steering
along a \emph{model-level} representation direction; we instead ask
whether an already-labeled \emph{SAE feature} direction is faithful to its
label, using their datasets/methodology as concept-detection benchmarks
(Precision/Recall/F1/AUROC) rather than as the primary steering
mechanism.

\section{Methods}
\paragraph{SAEs.} All SAEs read \texttt{blocks.$L$.hook\_resid\_pre}, with BOS excluded from training and splicing.
The baseline is 108 SAEs (12 layers $\times$ ReLU/TopK/sigmoid-surrogate JumpReLU $\times$ 3 seeds), width 3,072,
trained on OWT at L0 $\approx$ 128, one uninterrupted 15k-step protocol. The improved ``R2'' SAEs are width 12,288,
trained on 100M streamed OWT tokens with the TopK recipe of \citet{gao2024scaling} (AuxK), BatchTopK
\citep{bussmann2024batchtopk} and ReLU, at layers 1, 8 and 11, plus a frozen random-decoder TopK control
\citep{korznikov2026random}. ``R4'' fine-tunes the R2 TopK SAEs with an output-KL term at fixed L0
\citep{braun2024e2e, karvonen2025kl}, against an MSE-only control. The public res-jb SAEs \citep{bloom2024gpt2} are an
external reference.
\paragraph{Sparsity matching.} Architectures are compared only when OWT L0 is within $\pm$10\,\% of 128; unmatched
runs are flagged and excluded from rankings.
\paragraph{Information.} Probes are fitted on \emph{original} activations of training rows and applied frozen to
reconstructions; re-fitted probes are trained on reconstructions of the same training rows. GoT uses the pooled
\texttt{cities}+\texttt{neg\_cities} sets (2,992 statements, city-grouped splits). References: norm-matched noise and
PCA fitted on OWT training activations.
\paragraph{Behaviour and causality.} The reconstruction replaces the activation at every non-BOS position; we report
$\Delta$CE and KL$(p_{\mathrm{orig}}\|p_{\mathrm{spliced}})$ on held-out OWT. For causality we use an equal-length persona
task (RepE-LM: one-token honest/dishonest swaps), locate where GPT-2 carries the persona effect by activation patching,
and decompose that effect into the part carried by the SAE-reconstructed activation difference and the part carried by
the error term.
\paragraph{Evaluation discipline.} Every experiment was pre-registered with file hashes; a single confirmatory TEST
pass (M5) was run once for OWT and GoT. RepE-LM has no evaluated test split yet, so honesty results are validation
findings.

\section{Experiments and Results}
\paragraph{Reconstruction degrades with depth.} OWT test explained variance is 0.93--0.95 at layers 0--2 and
0.76--0.82 at layer 11 for the baseline SAEs.
\paragraph{Reconstruction and behaviour can be improved (TEST).} Against the baseline, R2 SAEs lower OWT NMSE by
30.8--73.7\,\% and spliced $\Delta$CE by 35.5--78.8\,\% at layers 1, 8 and 11, on every seed pair (M5). At layer 8,
TopK NMSE falls from 0.160 to 0.094 and $\Delta$CE from 0.133 to 0.062 nats. R4 lowers $\Delta$CE by a further
16--17\,\% at fixed L0, with a small NMSE regression (+2\,\%).
\paragraph{Truth information does not follow (TEST).} On GoT, no comparison changes the re-fitted probe gap (0/34
cells). The frozen original probe's drop on reconstructions (0.19--0.32 AUROC) is largely reproduced by norm-matched
noise; re-fitted probes lose 0.03--0.11 AUROC. At equal OWT reconstruction error PCA keeps at least as much truth
information as the SAEs; that comparison uses 298--519 PCA components against about 128 active SAE latents
(Section~\ref{sec:pca-note}).
\paragraph{Persona and causal faithfulness (VALIDATION).} The SAE-reconstructed activation difference carries
93--97\,\% of the persona effect at the layer-1 persona token but only 62--68\,\% at the layer-8 and layer-11 final
token. R2 raises this to 0.726 for TopK at layer 8 (from 0.625). The persona effect itself is small (0.022 nats at
layer 8). R4 lowers causal preservation at layer 11 (0.694 $\to$ 0.671).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:pca-note}
Faithfulness is level-specific. Better reconstruction reliably improves behavioural faithfulness, but it does not
make an independent reader recover more truth information, and a behaviour-targeted fine-tune can cost causal
preservation. Three limitations bound these conclusions. First, the GoT comparisons have limited sensitivity: seed
variability (sd 0.004--0.024) is as large as the observed differences, so a null result there is not evidence of
preservation. Second, the PCA reference is matched on reconstruction error, not on capacity; a capacity-matched
comparison is reported in the paper. Third, the SAE-selected feature patches keep the error term, which makes them
mathematically equivalent to adding an input-dependent decoder-space vector to the raw activation; they show that the
SAE selects directions that carry the effect, not that the effect is mediated by the SAE.

\section{Conclusion}
Training larger SAEs on more data with a modern recipe improves reconstruction and behavioural faithfulness on held-out
data, but truth information measured by independent probes does not improve, and the honesty and causal gains are so
far validation findings. Reconstruction metrics should therefore not be used as a proxy for information or causal
faithfulness. Next steps: the frozen RepE-LM test pass, task-level sparsity matching and the Gemma-2-2B extension.
