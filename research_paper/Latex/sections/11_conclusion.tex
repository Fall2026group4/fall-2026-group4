\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Sparse autoencoders can reconstruct language-model activations well without preserving everything the model uses.
Within a layer, reconstruction error predicts behavioural damage but not robustly the loss of truth information.
Information conclusions depend on the reader and on the comparison constraint. The reconstruction preserves a located
causal effect well at an early site and only about two-thirds of it at later sites. Better training improves
reconstruction and behaviour on held-out data without a confirmed gain in truth information, and behaviour-targeted
training is a trade-off. Public Gemma Scope SAEs show the same behavioural pattern in Gemma-2-2B. Reconstruction quality
is necessary but insufficient evidence of faithfulness, and SAE evaluations should report reconstruction, information,
behavioural and causal evidence separately, naming the reader, the reference constraint and the evidence status of each
claim.
