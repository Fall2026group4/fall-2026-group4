\section{Experimental Details}\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Datasets and splits.} OWT: 1{,}000 documents from \texttt{Skylion007/openwebtext} (streaming shuffle with
seed 42, 100--20{,}000 characters, exact deduplication), split 800/100/100 by document and truncated to 128 tokens,
giving 101{,}600 training, 12{,}700 validation and 12{,}700 test tokens excluding BOS. The scaled SAEs use 100M further
tokens from a shard that excludes every document in this sample. Geometry of Truth: \texttt{cities} and
\texttt{neg\_cities}, 1{,}496 statements each, split by city into 1{,}046/224/226 per set; the training split is further
divided into city groups A (training objectives) and B (evaluation probes). The numerical sets have a surface shortcut
(the second number alone gives AUROC 0.84) and are used only as a diagnostic. RepE-LM: 506 statements (train 417,
validation 89, and a separate held-out test split) times 20 prompt variants. Templates T1--T4 swap truthful/deceptive,
trustworthy/dishonest, sincere/lying and facts/lies, each with an honest-synonym control; T5--T6 are held-out wordings;
N1--N2 swap neutral words (tall/short, red/blue). Seven pre-registered construction gates verify the task
(Table~\ref{tab:m1}); every shortcut feature, including length, has AUROC 0.50. The Pile: validation file of
\texttt{monology/pile-uncopyrighted} (pinned revision and SHA-256); a seeded permutation (seed 42) over document indices
selects the first 512 documents with at least 32 tokens as the evaluation set and the next 64 as a disjoint reference
set for the mean-ablation vector; each document is tokenized with BOS and truncated to 1{,}024 tokens. The Pile test
file was never downloaded.

\paragraph{Base SAE training.} Each base SAE was trained in one uninterrupted run of 15{,}000 Adam steps (learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$, batch 256 sampled with replacement, gradient clipping at 10), about 38 passes over the training
tokens. A multiplicative controller adjusted the sparsity coefficient every 25 steps towards L0 = 128 for ReLU and
JumpReLU (surrogate temperature $T=0.1$); TopK fixes L0 by construction. Decoder columns were renormalised to unit norm
after every step. ReLU runs 2--6\,\% above the target L0; a within-layer adjustment using the L0 elasticity measured in
an earlier sweep changes no ranking except two near-ties. Of 3{,}072 latents, ReLU keeps 2{,}920--3{,}063 alive, TopK
1{,}711--2{,}851 and JumpReLU 469--2{,}941.

\paragraph{Scaled SAEs and behaviour-targeted fine-tune.} The scaled SAEs (TopK with an auxiliary dead-latent loss
\citep{gao2024scaling}, BatchTopK \citep{bussmann2024batchtopk} and ReLU controlled to L0 = 128; width 12{,}288; three
seeds) were trained on activations streamed from GPT-2, with the encoder initialised to the decoder transpose, input
scaling folded into the weights and cosine decay; 39 R2 runs were evaluated: 36 trained SAEs and 3
frozen-random-decoder baselines, run in 5.5 hours on one NVIDIA A10G. A TopK width
ablation at 3{,}072 latents separates data and recipe from width (on validation, equal-width retraining accounts for
23--35\,\% of the NMSE reduction). A modern straight-through JumpReLU did not settle at L0 = 128 in a pilot and was
abandoned before the full run. A comparison is an improvement only if all nine new/old seed pairs favour the new SAE and
the Welch 95\,\% interval excludes zero. The behaviour-targeted fine-tune trains the scaled TopK SAEs for 5M tokens at
learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$ with NMSE plus $\alpha\,\mathrm{KL}(p_{\text{orig}}\|p_{\text{spliced}})$, $\alpha$ set by
first-batch scale matching.

\paragraph{Dead-latent audit.} As a diagnostic requested during the course, we counted dead latents, defined as
latents that never fire ($z>0$) on the 12{,}700-token OWT validation set. All 108 base SAEs and all 36 trained scaled
SAEs were audited; the 3 frozen-random-decoder baselines were audited separately and are not counted as trained SAEs.
Per-run values are in \nolinkurl{results/shared/dead_latent_audit/dead_latents_by_sae.csv}. Some base families have
substantial dead fractions, especially JumpReLU and TopK at early layers; the worst run is the unmatched layer-0
JumpReLU run (seed 42), with about 95.7\,\% of its latents dead. Every trained scaled SAE has at most 0.36\,\% dead
latents on this set. The scaled TopK SAEs at the base width of 3{,}072 have none, so the reduction cannot be explained
by increased width alone; data, training duration and recipe changed together, so we do not attribute it to any one
factor. Fewer dead latents does not by itself imply better information, behavioural or causal faithfulness.

\paragraph{Truth-focused fine-tuning.} Every fine-tune starts from a base SAE and runs 500 Adam steps at learning rate
$10^{-5}$ with the sparsity controller continued from the base coefficient. Guards: OWT L0 within $\pm5\,\%$ of the base,
OWT EV at most 0.005 below the base (output-KL: 0.02 below Method A), spliced $\Delta$CE at most $1.10\times$ the base,
and no loss of the truth preference beyond 0.018. A first truth-aware objective distilled a linear probe's logits and
appeared to remove most of the truth gap; an audit found that the evaluation probe was the training teacher (same rows,
labels and weights). In a corrected design (teacher fitted on group A, probes on group B, random-teacher control) that
objective was worse than plain reconstruction fine-tuning on every seed (TopK group-B MLP gap 0.189 versus 0.107; random
teacher 0.165). Under output-KL fine-tuning, OWT L0 rose by 3.5--8.1\,\% for ReLU and JumpReLU, while TopK is
fixed at 128 (Table~\ref{tab:impl_l0}).

\section{Statistical Procedures and Sensitivity}\label{app:stats}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Within-layer reanalysis.} Fixed-effects regressions (layer and architecture fixed effects, log L0) with
        Freedman--Lane permutation within layer (5{,}000 permutations), bootstrap intervals (2{,}000) and Holm
        correction over four primary tests.
  \item \textbf{Information.} Statement-grouped paired bootstrap, one-sided tests against noise and PCA references,
        Benjamini--Hochberg correction across SAEs per task; a 0.02 AUROC tolerance defines ``preserved''.
  \item \textbf{Causal.} Holm correction over 65 cells; 200 matched-norm random vectors per cell; synonym and neutral
        controls; for latent patches, 200 random latent sets matched on activation frequency and magnitude.
  \item \textbf{Fine-tuning.} Paired by seed; mean, sd, per-seed values and a 95\,\% $t$-interval with two degrees of
        freedom. The ``consistent'' rule (all three seeds and $|$mean$|>2$\,sd) is not equivalent to that interval. No
        correction is applied across the 9--12 cells of a criterion.
  \item \textbf{Capacity-matched PCA.} Per seed, a paired statement-grouped bootstrap (1{,}000 resamples) of SAE gap minus
        PCA gap; a cell is better or worse only if every seed's interval excludes zero. An approximate post-hoc Holm
        correction is reported alongside.
  \item \textbf{Minimum detectable effect} (MDE $=2.80\times$SE at two-sided $\alpha=0.05$ and power 0.80): one SAE's
        truth re-fit gap on test 0.030--0.048 AUROC; between-family truth comparisons about 0.05--0.09 AUROC; one SAE's
        causal preservation share 0.013--0.017 at layer 1, 0.036--0.069 at layer 11 and 0.054--0.107 at layer 8.
  \item \textbf{Gemma.} One pretrained SAE per layer; 95\,\% percentile intervals from 1{,}000 document resamples (seed
        0), with a Bonferroni (98.33\,\%) sensitivity interval for H-G1.
  \item \textbf{What the confirmatory intervals cover.} The pre-registered comparison rules use Welch tests over three
        seeds per arm on one fixed evaluation set, so they generalise over SAE training seeds, not over statements or
        documents. The sensitivity analysis S1 (Appendix~\ref{sec:audit}) adds the cluster-sampling variance (paired
        cluster bootstrap, 2{,}000 resamples; degrees of freedom the smaller of Satterthwaite and Welch, so the interval is
        never narrower than the seed-only one).
\end{itemize}
Raw MSE is never averaged across layers, and MSE improvement is $100\times(\text{baseline}-\text{improved})/\text{baseline}$.

\section{Audit Sensitivity Analyses}\label{sec:audit}

After an independent audit of the code and results we ran six pre-registered sensitivity analyses on validation rows
only (no test row read; \texttt{results/\allowbreak{}shared/\allowbreak{}audit\_sensitivity/}). None is a new
confirmatory test, and none changes a frozen verdict.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{S1, statement and document uncertainty.} The seed-only intervals of the OWT and truth test pass and of
        the RepE-LM test omit the sampling of documents or statements, which accounts for 33--99\,\% of each
        comparison's variance on validation. Adding it, HT2 holds in all nine cells, but the TopK cells of HT1 (adjusted
        95\,\% CI $[+0.014, +0.240]$) and HT3 ($[-0.093, -0.002]$) no longer pass Holm over the 13 cells (adjusted
        $p=0.156$ for both). Sixteen of 18 reconstruction and behaviour cells of the OWT test pass hold; the exceptions
        are the small layer-1 $\Delta$CE gains of ReLU and of BatchTopK over JumpReLU.
  \item \textbf{S2, persona versus neutral.} Normalised by decodable headroom, no layer shows a persona-specific loss;
        the neutral one-word contrast loses a significantly larger share at layers 5 and 7--11. The earlier reading that
        part of the persona loss is persona-specific is withdrawn.
  \item \textbf{S3, matched task-level L0.} Truncating every SAE to the smallest natural task-level L0 among the
        compared arms leaves 54 of 57 scaled-versus-base comparison classes unchanged, including the layer-8 causal gain
        of BatchTopK over JumpReLU, but changes the ordering of the base architectures in 13 of 19 metric--layer cells.
  \item \textbf{S4, pooled truth task.} Text-only baselines are at chance (AUROC at most 0.55). Pooling
        \texttt{cities} with \texttt{neg\_cities} makes the label truth combined with negation: within one polarity a
        linear probe reads truth from layer 1 (0.75--0.77), while the pooled probe stays at 0.52--0.59 at layers 1--2.
        Low pooled AUROC at early layers is therefore not evidence that truth is absent (a post-hoc reading).
  \item \textbf{S5, noise references.} The SAE's own error, reassigned at random to other statements, reproduces only
        50--67\,\% of the frozen logistic truth drop at layers 6--11, so 33--50\,\% of that drop is aligned with the
        input and its label (92 of 99 cells; Section~\ref{sec:info}). The frozen truth-reader drop is therefore partly
        generic reader sensitivity and partly label-aligned SAE error.
  \item \textbf{S6, probe regularisation.} An 11-value regularisation grid changes no comparison class, and no probe
        selects a grid edge.
\end{itemize}

\section{Causal Analysis Details}\label{app:causal}

\paragraph{Decomposition.} At a site, $\Delta=x_H-x_U$, $\Delta_{\text{rec}}=\hat{x}_H-\hat{x}_U$ and
$\Delta_{\text{err}}=e_H-e_U$, so $\Delta=\Delta_{\text{rec}}+\Delta_{\text{err}}$ exactly. With
$T(v)=\mathrm{KL}(p_H\|p_U)-\mathrm{KL}(p_H\|p_{U+v})$ we define $T_{\text{full}}=T(\Delta)$,
$T_{\text{rec}}=T(\Delta_{\text{rec}})$, $T_{\text{err}}=T(\Delta_{\text{err}})$, the interaction
$I=T_{\text{full}}-T_{\text{rec}}-T_{\text{err}}$ and Shapley shares $\phi_{\text{rec}}=(T_{\text{rec}}+T_{\text{full}}-T_{\text{err}})/2$,
which are not interpreted additively when the interaction is large. All exact checks pass ($\Delta$ identity error
$9.5\times10^{-7}$; $T_{\text{full}}$ reproduces the site sweep to $7\times10^{-8}$).

\paragraph{Why the patches are not mediation.} Because the error term is kept, every patch has the form $x_U+v$ with
$v=W_d(z'-z_U)$, and the decoder bias cancels. $T_{\text{rec}}$ adds $v=W_d(z_H-z_U)$ and a top-$k$ patch adds
$W_{d,S}(z_{H,S}-z_{U,S})$, so each is identical to adding one vector to the dishonest prompt's activation. The same holds
for any encode--edit--decode intervention that keeps the error, including input-dependent clamps:
$x'=x+W_d(z'(x)-z(x))$. Because a dictionary of 3{,}072 or 12{,}288 latents spans the residual stream, lying in decoder
space does not restrict $v$. The only SAE-specific ingredient is how $v$ is chosen, namely the encoder's input-dependent
latent selection, which the matched random-latent and isotropic controls test. Establishing mediation would require the
downstream computation to pass through SAE nonlinearities, for example with SAEs spliced at later layers or without the
error term. A layer-6 clamp experiment of that kind found no supported effect (\evlabel{validation}).

\paragraph{Site-sweep controls.} A same-meaning synonym swap travels the same route as the persona swap at about half
the size, and the persona-minus-synonym difference is preserved in the reconstruction at every site. Template T1
(truthful/deceptive) has no effect beyond its synonym control and serves as a control case, so T2--T4 form the primary
set. The instruction words after the persona slot carry up to 0.28 of the effect.

\section{Gemma-2-2B Details}\label{app:m6}

\paragraph{Validity gates.} All five gates passed before any result was read: sample integrity (data SHA-256 and
tokenizer revision match the pre-registration; 512 + 64 disjoint documents); hook equivalence (the hooked activation
equals the model's hidden state in all 1{,}536 checks); determinism and identity (identity-splice and repeat-pass mean KL
exactly 0.0); SAE sanity (measured L0 69.5, 84.8 and 75.1 against release values 68, 82 and 73); and no substitution of
the pinned SAEs.

\paragraph{Known limitations.} The logits are computed in bf16 as pre-registered. A smoke-run measurement on eight
documents found a mean KL from an fp32 output head of 0.00083 and a mean absolute CE difference of 0.026 nats per
prediction; this floor is small next to the SAE effect on KL but was not repeated on the full run, and its effect on the
signed paired differences was not measured. Gemma's pretraining data are not public, so Gemma may have seen some of these
documents; this affects absolute cross-entropy but not the paired comparisons. Loss recovered (0.98--0.99) is high only
because mean ablation is very destructive (about 9 nats).

\paragraph{Per-source variation and reproducibility.} Six Pile sources have at least 20 evaluation documents (457
documents); the SAE-to-random $\Delta$CE ratio exceeds 1 in each of them at every layer, but no per-source test was
pre-registered, so this is descriptive only. The run used code commit \texttt{13eaeca} from a clean tree on one Tesla T4
GPU (no native bf16) with strict deterministic algorithms, PyTorch 2.7.0 and transformers 5.21.0, took 8{,}067.5\,s and
peaked at 8.58\,GB of GPU memory. Model and tokenizer were pinned to revision \texttt{c5ebcd40} of
\texttt{google/gemma-2-2b}, SAEs to revision \texttt{fd571b47} of \texttt{google/gemma-scope-2b-pt-res}, and data to
revision \texttt{3be90335} of \texttt{monology/pile-uncopyrighted}.

\section{Leakage Controls and Test Exposure}\label{app:exposure}

OWT is split by document and truth statements by city, so no document or city appears in two splits. Training objectives
use city group A only, and evaluation probes are fitted on group B. The Gemma evaluation and reference documents are
disjoint, and the mean-ablation vector uses the reference set only. The OWT test split was used for the reconstruction
table and once more in the pre-registered confirmatory pass. The truth test split was scored for all 108 base SAEs with
frozen probes and twice for historical layer-6 fine-tunes before that pass, so it is treated as previously exposed. The
RepE-LM test split was evaluated once, under its frozen protocol (Section~\ref{sec:h1test}), and is now spent. The
truth-subspace experiment used cross-fitted folds of the training and validation cities only and never loaded the
cities test split.
